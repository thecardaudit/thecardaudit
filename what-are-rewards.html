What Are Rewards, Actually?

3-minute read Cash back, points, and travel — in plain English.

Look, most business owners I talk to have the same setup: one card, everything goes on it, and at the end of the year they get a little statement credit and call it good. That’s fine. It’s just leaving money on the table, and once you see how it works, it’s hard to unsee.

Here’s the whole idea in one sentence: the credit card companies will pay you to spend money you’re already spending. That’s it. That’s the game.

Cash back is the simple one

You spend a dollar, the card gives you back a penny or two. A flat-rate card on $10,000 a month is $200 back, every month, $2,400 a year, for doing nothing different. The catch is the flat cards treat every dollar the same. Category cards don’t. Gas, ads, restaurants, phone bills — the right card pays several times more on those, and that’s where the real money is. You’re not spending more. You’re just using a different piece of plastic for the gas than you use for the software subscription.

Points are the same thing, with an upgrade

Points and miles are just cash back wearing a nicer outfit. One point is usually worth a penny if you cash it out. Boring, but safe. The fun part is you don’t have to cash it out. You can move those points to an airline or a hotel, and suddenly that penny is worth two or three cents. Same points, same spending, worth double or triple — if you use them for travel.

That’s not a trick. The airlines and hotels want your points, so they give you a better deal for them than cash does. It’s the one part of this whole thing that actually feels like cheating, and it’s completely above board.

A real example

Say you run a small business and you put $8,000 a month through your cards. On a flat-rate card, that’s $960 a year back. Now say you split it up — the right card for fuel, the right card for ads, the right one for travel — and you use the right card for each category instead. That’s $2,880 a year. Same spending, $1,920 more, just from sorting it.

Now take those points and use them for travel instead of cash. That $2,880 is 288,000 points. Move them to Hyatt and they cover 7 nights at Secrets in St. Lucia for 180,000 points — all-inclusive, room, food, and drinks. That still leaves you 108,000 points to put toward flights.

Nobody’s getting rich off this. But a free week at an all-inclusive in St. Lucia, plus points left over for the flights to get there, for spending you were going to do anyway? That’s real money.

That’s really all there is to it. You’re already spending the money. The only question is whether the card you’re using is paying you back for it.

Intro Offers: The Bonus Is Real. The Catch Is Real Too.

2-minute read The spending number, the everyday rate, and the fine print.

Every few months a card comes out with a number so big it stops you mid-scroll. Two hundred thousand points for opening a card. That sounds like free money, and in a way it is. But nobody is handing out two hundred thousand points out of kindness, and the catch is worth knowing before you sign up.

Here is how an intro offer actually works. You open the card, and the bank gives you a few months — usually three — to spend a set amount. Hit the number, and the points land in your account. Miss it, and they don’t. It is a one-time deal. You get it once, and then it is gone.

That is the first thing to check: can you hit the spending number without changing how you spend? A card I looked at recently offers up to 200,000 points if you spend $15,000 in the first three months. For a business already spending that much, that is a genuinely good deal. But a cleaning business I talked to spends about $12,000 on ads in three months. That is $3,000 short. She would either miss the bonus or go find $3,000 of spending she did not need. Never buy things you do not need to chase a bonus. The math never works out in your favor.

The second thing: the bonus ends, and the everyday rate does not. The bonus is a one-time payment. The rate your card pays on fuel, or advertising, or supplies — that is what pays you every month, for years. A card with a giant bonus and a weak everyday rate will lose to a card with a smaller bonus and a strong rate by the second year. The bonus is the headline. The everyday rate is the story.

The third thing: the fine print has a say too. The points usually show up weeks after you hit the spending — sometimes two or three months later. And when a bank says “up to” 200,000 points, that means your offer might be lower. You find out exactly what you are getting before you accept, so read that screen, not the ad.

So when is an intro offer a great idea? When three things are true. You were already going to spend that much anyway. The card also fits the way your business actually spends, so it keeps paying after the bonus is gone. And you would have wanted the card even without the bonus.

That is the order that matters. Pick the card for what it pays you every day. Treat the bonus as the extra. The bonus is a nice way to start. It is a terrible reason to pick a card.

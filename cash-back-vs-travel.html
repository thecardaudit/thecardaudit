Cash Back or Travel Rewards: Which One Is Actually Worth More?

3-minute read Same spending, two very different payouts.

Every business owner I talk to asks me the same question, usually right after I show them what their spending could be earning: “Should I take the cash back, or the travel rewards?”

Fair question. And the honest answer is: it depends on what you would actually do with the points. Because the same spending can be worth two very different amounts depending on which way you take it.

Let me show you with real numbers.

Say your business spends about $8,000 a month. On a flat everyday card, that comes back as around $960 a year. Sort that same spending by category — fuel on one card, advertising on another, the rest where it earns best — and it comes to around $2,880 a year in rewards. Same spending, same business. The only thing that changed is which cards it went on.

Now here is where the cash-versus-travel question comes in. That $2,880 is the cash value. Take it as cash, and $2,880 lands in your account. But use those same points for flights and hotels, and they stretch further — roughly double. That same year of spending can cover a week at an all-inclusive resort in St. Lucia, with points left over toward the flights.

So which one is worth more? On paper, travel — by a lot. In real life, it depends on you.

Cash back is simple. The money shows up, you spend it on whatever you want, and you never have to think about flights, blackout dates, or point charts. If you do not travel much, or you just want the money, cash back is the right call. There is nothing wrong with simple. Simple is the reason most people pick it.

Travel rewards are worth more, but only if you use them. They are worth the most to people who travel anyway — for work, for family trips, for a vacation you were already going to take. A contractor I talked to recently spends $4,000 a month on fuel per truck. On fuel alone, the right card could be worth around $1,900 a year in rewards per truck as cash — or roughly $3,800 a year if he uses them for travel instead. He told me he gets more out of miles than cash back, because he actually travels. For him, travel wins by a mile.

But travel rewards are only worth something if you go somewhere. Points sitting in an account are worth nothing. If you never fly, never stay in hotels, and the idea of planning a trip around points sounds like homework, take the cash. The best reward is the one you will actually use.

That is why the first question I ask in an audit is not about cards. It is about you: cash back, travel rewards, or not sure yet? The answer decides everything else. A business that wants cash gets a different stack than a business that wants to fly to St. Lucia. Same spending, different destination.

So here is the short version. Cash back is simple and predictable. Travel is worth roughly double — if you travel. And the wrong card is the one that pays you the smaller number for the way you actually live.
